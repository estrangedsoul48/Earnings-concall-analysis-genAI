Executive summary. FY2026 was a strong foundational year for Affle 3i, delivering 19.5% revenue growth and 26.3% EBITDA growth — precisely in line with its publicly stated internal KPI of combined revenue + EBITDA growth of ~45%. The business is genuinely compounding: 13 consecutive quarters of sequential topline growth, a near-pristine balance sheet with ₹1,792 Cr of net cash, and operating cash flows growing at 25% CAGR over five years. The critical analytical question is whether elevated inventory and data costs (now 62–63% of revenue vs. a historical 60–61%) represent a temporary, value-creating investment or a structural gross-margin reset. Management's answer is internally consistent and partially verifiable — but requires monitoring. The promoter warrant issuance of ₹11 billion for potential M&A introduces both an exciting strategic catalyst and a meaningful governance question.

Revenue quality is genuine. Growth is fully organic — no acquisitions since FY2023. Both volume (CPCU conversions up 16% YoY in Q4) and pricing (CPCU rate ₹57.8 → ₹60.0) are contributing, and geographic growth is broad-based with India + Emerging Markets at +21.2% and Developed Markets at +18.0%. There is no evidence of channel stuffing, related-party inflation, or inventory-led revenue booking — the CPCU model is inherently performance-tied and self-disciplining.

The gross margin compression is the central analytical puzzle. Inventory and data costs rose 270 bps YoY in Q4 (to 63.3% of revenue), expanding further from the already-elevated levels seen across the last six quarters. Management characterises roughly 10% of this cost line as an investment in verticalization intelligence for international markets — expensed immediately under accounting standards but economically akin to R&D capitalisation. If management's framing is correct, the "true" gross margin is approximately 250–300 bps higher than reported, which would place EBITDA margins closer to 24.5–25%. The thesis is plausible — Affle has 21 years of disciplined execution — but independently unverifiable.

PAT growth (16%) lagging EBITDA growth (20.3%) in Q4 is explained by a base-period tax rate anomaly (Q4 FY25 benefited from a lower effective tax rate of 16.8% vs 19.3% this quarter). On a full-year basis, tax rates are comparable (18.6% vs 18.3%), and the PAT margin expanded marginally to 16.3%. Depreciation growing at 25.8% YoY in Q4 — faster than revenue — reflects amortisation of intangibles from the 2020–23 acquisition wave, which will taper as those intangibles age.

Balance sheet is fortress-grade. Net cash (cash minus borrowings) stands at approximately ₹1,780 Cr (₹17,802 mn) — representing 87% of market cap at the current ₹20,501 Cr valuation. Borrowings of just ₹119 mn are negligible. There are no pledged promoter shares, no off-balance-sheet entities, no guarantees flagged, and no covenant risk. The balance sheet could absorb a meaningful acquisition entirely from internal resources without any equity dilution — making the promoter warrant issuance a strategic choice rather than a necessity.

Goodwill and intangibles at ₹15,175 mn (34.3% of total assets) are a structural feature from the 2020–23 acquisition phase. This is the primary non-cash risk: if acquired entities underperform, impairments become possible. However, management explicitly states all acquired entities have been converted to the CPCU business model and are cash-generative — an assertion consistent with the strong OCF trajectory. No impairment charges have been flagged.

Working capital appears clean. The Q3 FY26 OCF/PAT compression to 75.8% (9 months) was attributed to extended collection cycles from advertising agencies undergoing periodic client audits. The Q4 normalisation — bringing full-year OCF/PAT to ~110% — validates this explanation. There is no evidence of receivable inflation, aggressive revenue recognition, or hidden stress in collections across the core business verticals.

The CPCU model is Affle's moat. 99.2% of revenue is earned only when a consumer converts — install, register, transact, subscribe. This outcome-linked model creates a self-reinforcing flywheel: advertisers only pay for results, which incentivises Affle to continually improve targeting quality, which improves ROI, which expands wallet share. No competitor listed on Indian exchanges operates at this scale on a pure CPCU model.

CPCU rate growth despite an increasing share of emerging-market revenue is the most analytically interesting development. Emerging markets (India + GEM) grew to 73.9% of revenue in Q3 FY26 (vs. 71.6% in Q4 FY26), and yet CPCU rates have continued rising. This is explained by three drivers: (1) iOS premium user targeting, where advertisers pay significantly more for high-LTV users; (2) deep vertical specialisation that allows Affle to charge a premium for sector-specific intelligence (healthcare patient personas, gaming high-value users); and (3) CTV inventory commanding higher rates than standard mobile display. The structural drift of the CPCU rate toward ₹60+ is a durable signal, not a blip.

Non-CPCU revenue is deliberately minimal. Management frames non-CPCU as a "top-of-funnel entry point" for customers who eventually migrate to CPCU. This is strategically honest — and confirms Affle is not chasing gross revenue at the expense of quality. The 0.8% non-CPCU share is a feature, not a bug.

Step 7
Industry & competitive positioning

Digital advertising tailwinds are structural and accelerating. Global digital ad spend continues to grow, with AI dramatically improving the measurability of returns — which shifts budget allocation away from brand/impression-based spend toward performance/ROI-linked models. Affle is structurally positioned at this intersection. The management's observation that "when budgets get tighter, CPCU models benefit" is counterintuitive but historically validated: performance advertising share increases during downturns as CFOs demand accountability.

The agentic AI opportunity is real and Affle is early. Management identifies two forward opportunities: (1) AI-powered apps needing user acquisition (spend on CPCU with Affle to grow) and (2) AI-powered apps needing monetisation (Affle helps them generate ad revenue). The "human vs. non-human distillation" capability — protecting advertisers from fraud in an agentic AI world — is a genuine moat that competitors like Trade Desk or DV360 have not explicitly addressed. Affle's pre-IPO patents on this problem (filed 2017) give it a multi-year head start.

Developed markets remain the key growth frontier. North America and Europe contribute ~27–28% of revenue despite Affle having negligible legacy penetration. The hire of Sameer Sondhi as North America CEO (25+ years in US ad tech) and the active M&A evaluation of 4 targets is the right strategy to solve this — organic scaling in developed markets is slow (as Affle's own experience shows). A well-executed acquisition that gives Affle an existing US sales team and customer relationships could accelerate the developed markets trajectory materially.

Key risk to monitor: Walled garden aggression. Meta, Google, and Apple's SKAdNetwork have historically compressed independent ad-tech intermediaries. Affle's defence is its CPCU model (which integrates directly with advertisers' first-party app data, bypassing the traditional auction entirely) and its focus on emerging markets where walled-garden reach is less dominant. But in developed markets, walled gardens have stronger positions — validating the need for Affle's differentiated premium/vertical approach rather than head-on volume competition.
Step 8
Capital allocation analysis

Historic track record on M&A is strong. Five acquisitions between 2020–2023 (Appnext, RevX, YouAppi, mediasmart, Jampp) were all below revenue multiples typical for the sector, all converted to the CPCU model, and all are cash-generative today. Management deliberately paused M&A for three years (FY2024–FY2026) to prove integration capability — a rare display of restraint that builds credibility.

The ₹11 billion warrant issuance is the key near-term capital allocation event. Affle Holdings (promoter) will acquire ~7.4 million warrants at ₹1,487 per share — fractionally above the current market price of ₹1,456. At full conversion, this injects ₹11 billion of fresh equity capital into Affle 3i's balance sheet, taking total firepower (existing cash + warrant proceeds) to approximately ₹2,900 Cr. This is the war chest being built for the announced "meaningfully sized" acquisition.

The governance concern is real but contextualised. Why warrants to the promoter rather than a QIP to institutional investors? Management's explanation — to "show seriousness" to selling boards and to maintain pricing flexibility as negotiations continue — is plausible. However, the promoter enriches itself if the stock rises above ₹1,487 before conversion, and the 25% upfront payment (₹275 Cr) is modest relative to the eventual dilution commitment. Institutional investors asked this question on the call and received a deflected answer. This warrants monitoring through shareholder vote approval.

Capital allocation scorecard
Capex discipline: Asset-light model. Capex is minimal — almost all investment is in human capital and data. No large physical infrastructure commitments.
Debt management: Borrowings reduced from ₹126 mn (FY25) to ₹119 mn (FY26). Balance sheet is effectively debt-free.
M&A playbook: Track record of buying at revenue multiples, not EBITDA multiples — conservative valuation anchoring. Committed to same discipline for next deal.
Dividend policy: Zero dividend despite ₹1,792 Cr net cash. Accumulating for M&A. Acceptable for a growth-phase company but zero yield with a 45x P/E is a high-trust requirement.

Warrant structure: Promoter acquires warrants at ₹1,487 with 25% upfront. If the stock falls materially, promoter may not convert — leaving Affle without anticipated M&A capital. Conditional firepower is less reliable than a completed QIP.
Step 9
Red flags & forensic detection
Minor Moderate Severe
Gross margin compression (moderate): Inventory and data costs have risen structurally from ~60% to 62–63% of revenue over 7–8 quarters. Management attributes ~10% of the cost line to "investment" — but this cannot be independently audited or disaggregated from reported financials. If this investment does not translate into CPCU rate improvement or conversion volume gains in FY27, the compression becomes structural, not cyclical. Monitor Q1–Q2 FY27 gross margins closely.
Promoter warrant structure (moderate): ₹11 billion warrants to be issued to Affle Holdings at ₹1,487. The source of funds for Affle Holdings was explicitly deflected on the Q4 call — "we will disclose if notified." This is a governance gap. If Affle Holdings is leveraged to fund the warrant subscription, the promoter's effective risk profile changes materially. Requires shareholder vote and subsequent disclosure.
Goodwill and intangibles concentration (moderate): ₹15,175 mn (34.3% of assets) from prior acquisitions. While no impairments have been flagged and management asserts all entities are cash-generative, a large future acquisition could add further goodwill. If the M&A cycle resumes at scale, this risk grows. D&A is already growing at 25.8% YoY — faster than revenue — pressuring PAT growth relative to EBITDA growth.
Other income decline (minor): FY2026 other income fell 16.5% to ₹782 mn from ₹938 mn in FY2025. Management attributes this to FX losses and lower interest income as some cash was deployed. At ₹1,792 Cr of net cash, this income line should partially recover as rates stabilise, but any significant deployment for M&A will further reduce it — moderately impacting PAT growth optics.
M&A execution risk (minor/elevated pending): Affle has been evaluating acquisitions for 6+ months (10 targets → 4 shortlisted). The longer this takes, the higher the risk of overpaying under competitive pressure or promotional board pressure. Management's "we will not rush" framing is reassuring, but the warrant issuance creates an implicit 18-month clock before options expire.
Real Money Gaming regulatory overhang (minor): The India RMG ban cost Affle ~₹10–12 Cr of quarterly revenue in the base period (modest at ~0.5% of quarterly revenue). This has been fully absorbed and replaced. However, Indian regulatory unpredictability in select verticals (Government category also saw pullback in Q3) remains a structural feature. Affle's vertical diversification is its primary hedge.
Forensic assessment: No signs of accounting manipulation, aggressive revenue recognition, inventory-led bookings, or cash flow manipulation. The 9M FY26 OCF concern was temporary and resolved. No auditor qualifications or sudden policy changes detected. The two meaningful risks — gross margin pressure and M&A governance — are well understood by the market, disclosed openly, and manageable. This is not a forensic red-flag situation; it is a company in a deliberate investment cycle.

The current valuation at ₹1,456 per share and 45x trailing P/E is not cheap, but it is not egregiously expensive for the quality on offer — particularly when adjusted for the ₹127/share of net cash. The path to value creation from here runs through two variables: (1) gross margin recovery as verticalization investments mature in FY27, and (2) a value-accretive M&A announcement that expands the developed markets footprint. Either one alone would re-rate the stock. Both together could drive a significant rerating. The appropriate posture for long-term institutional investors is to accumulate on dips toward ₹1,300–1,350, where the forward P/E compresses to ~33–35x and the margin of safety becomes more comfortable. At the current price, the stock is fully priced but not overpriced — the entry is conditional rather than urgent. Size positions proportionate to comfort with the M&A uncertainty, and use Q1 FY27 results as the first material checkpoint on gross margin trajectory.
