Executive Snapshot

Verdict in one line: FY26 was Artemis's strongest year on record — volume growth, ARPOB expansion, and operating leverage converged into the best quarterly margin (21.3% in Q4) in company history, with the business now structurally above the 20% EBITDA threshold. The financial architecture is clean, debt is declining, and free cash flow turned meaningfully positive. The critical question is not whether the Gurgaon engine fires — it does — but whether the Raipur ramp-up and South Delhi execution will be handled without balance sheet stress.

What actually happened this quarter

Q4 FY26 delivered across every metric that matters. Consolidated revenue grew 16.4% YoY to ₹279 Cr, EBITDA grew 23.1% to ₹59 Cr, and PAT grew 32.1% to ₹30 Cr. Critically, EBITDA margins expanded 116 bps YoY to 21.3% — the highest-ever quarterly print. Volume growth at Gurgaon was 9.2%, ARPOB was ₹84,571 (+7.3%), and occupancy hit 64.6%. The quarter was fundamentally strong, not optically inflated. Finance costs declined 18.6% YoY as IFC loan repayments progressed, creating a double tailwind of revenue expansion and cost compression at the interest line.

One nuance investors should note: Q3 FY26 had a ₹3.07 Cr one-time exceptional item (gratuity and leave encashment provision) that makes the QoQ PAT jump (36.2%) look larger than the underlying trend. Adjusting for this, the organic sequential improvement is still impressive at ~20–25% QoQ — confirmation that Q4 was genuinely the strongest quarter operationally.

Top 5 developments

  • ✓

  • ◐

  • ✓

What the market is likely missing

Three things the market underweights: (1) The corporate cost allocation effect — today, all corporate overhead sits entirely on Gurgaon. As Raipur and South Delhi come online, this cost gets distributed, mechanically boosting Gurgaon's standalone margin by 150–200 bps without any operational improvement. (2) The IFC relationship — IFC does not take 12% stakes in marginal businesses. Their entry signals institutional confidence in the expansion plan and likely comes with governance improvements and international patient referral network access. (3) ARPOB trajectory — management targets 7–8% annual ARPOB growth primarily through case mix shift, not pricing. At current volumes, each 100-bps of ARPOB growth translates to ~₹10–12 Cr incremental EBITDA annually.

Result Quality Analysis

Revenue Growth Dissection

Revenue growth of 15.4% YoY for FY26 is entirely organic — no acquisitions, no inorganic additions. The growth decomposes neatly into two layers: volume (IP volumes +9.6% YoY at Gurgaon) and realisation (ARPOB +7.8%). This is the ideal growth composition for a hospital — it means both the hospital is filling beds faster and getting paid more per bed. The Gurgaon standalone grew faster (17.8% revenue, 17.2% EBITDA) than the consolidated entity, confirming the subsidiary drag is diminishing.

The payor mix evolution is structurally positive. International revenue has risen from ~29% in H1 FY25 to 31–32% in FY26. Insured (TPA) has been held broadly stable at 30–31%. Government business (at lower realisation rates) has been managed down from 19–20% toward 16–18%. This payor mix improvement is the primary driver of ARPOB expansion — international patients realise at 1.3x domestic ARPOB.

The margin expansion story has three distinct drivers. First, revenue operating leverage — the hotel-like cost structure of hospitals means fixed costs (building depreciation, senior clinical staff, corporate overhead) are absorbed faster as occupancy rises from 60% to 65%+. Second, consumption efficiency — management explicitly targets 1.5–2% consumption cost reduction annually, which has been delivered. Third, declining finance cost — IFC debt repayment has reduced interest burden by ₹425 Lacs YoY in FY26, which flows entirely to PBT. At the PAT level, the leverage is even more pronounced because base PAT was low.

Tax Anomaly — Watch Item

Total tax in Q4 FY26 grew 73.7% YoY vs PAT growth of only 32.1%. The effective tax rate appears to be rising. This could reflect deferred tax liability build-up (accelerated depreciation unwind) or MAT credit exhaustion. The CFO confirmed no specific one-offs in tax — this warrants deeper investigation in the annual report. A rising effective tax rate in future quarters would compress PAT margins even if EBITDA remains healthy.

The working capital structure of Artemis is intrinsically favorable. Hospitals in India collect from cash patients upfront and from insurance companies within 30–60 days on average. Government patients create receivables (30–45 days), but Artemis keeps government mix capped at 16–18%. The result is negative net current assets — the business funds itself from customer advances, not bank lines. This is a structural moat that many analysts overlook.

Balance Sheet Stress Mapping — The ₹22,885 Lacs Anomaly

The most important hidden item in FY26 results: "Other Non-Current Assets" jumped from ₹3,345 Lacs in FY25 to ₹22,885 Lacs in FY26 — a ₹19,540 Lac (195 Cr) surge. Simultaneously, cash and bank balances fell from ₹39,554 Lacs to ₹20,614 Lacs — a ₹18,940 Lac decline. This is not a coincidence. The company has deployed IFC funds into project-related advances and deposits for the South Delhi and Raipur projects. This is capital committed ahead of revenue generation — economically sound but creates a "cash consumed" optic that can confuse P&L-focused investors. The real balance sheet is not stressed; the cash is not lost — it is deposited as security against long-term operating leases.

Debt Maturity & Liquidity

Total consolidated debt of ₹226 Cr against cash of ₹206 Cr implies near-zero net debt. The IFC loan (originally at attractive development finance rates) has been systematically repaid — finance cost fell 13.3% YoY in FY26 despite broadly stable debt levels in H1. The debt-to-equity ratio of 1.16 at H1 FY26 (as confirmed by management) is comfortable. Peak debt forecast of ₹350 Cr implies debt-to-equity of ~0.35x on projected FY27 equity — entirely manageable. No promoter pledging is disclosed.

Off-Balance-Sheet Items

The South Delhi lease is a significant off-balance-sheet commitment — a 30-year lease on ~3.5 acres in South Delhi with ~7,00,000 sqft built-up area, monetised over 30 years. This will come on-balance-sheet as a right-of-use asset under Ind AS 116 once operationalised. The advance payments against building milestones will be adjusted against rent over 8–10 years post-commissioning. The Raipur arrangement similarly involves a 30-year lease with the land and building owned by partners — the P&L stays with Artemis, but so does the lease obligation. These are not sinister structures; they are sensible capital-light models.

The specialty mix reveals a deliberate high-complexity pivot. Oncology (18%) + Neuro (15%) + Orthopaedics (13%) = 46% of revenue from the three highest-acuity, highest-margin specialties. Industry & Competitive Positioning

Industry Cycle Position

The Indian private hospital sector is in a structural, multi-year upcycle driven by four convergent forces: rising health insurance penetration (CGHS revision improving private hospital viability), growing domestic affluence and willingness to pay for premium care, medical value travel from 100+ countries (India's ARPOB is 3–5x cheaper than Southeast Asia for equivalent quality), and an aging population driving chronic disease management volumes. The sector is experiencing simultaneous price improvement (case mix shift) and volume growth — a rare combination that typically occurs only in early-to-mid cycle expansion phases. Artemis is a pure-play beneficiary of all four forces.

Competitive Positioning in NCR

Artemis occupies a distinctive positioning in Delhi-NCR: it claims the highest ARPOB in Gurgaon, exceeding even South Delhi hospitals, achieved not through aggressive pricing (it is priced at or below Medanta, below Fortis/Max) but through case complexity, international patient mix, and bed turnaround efficiency. This is a clinically-earned premium — a moat that is more durable than brand-alone premiums. The 1st JCI-accredited hospital in Gurgaon distinction, combined with 14 Centres of Excellence, creates a referral network effect that compounds over time.

Regulatory Environment — GIC Council (Key Structural Tailwind)

Dr. Chakravarty is personally heading the working group within CII on hospital categorization and pricing reform, working directly with IRDAI's Ajay Seth. Five working groups are addressing: (1) transparent hospital categorization; (2) ethical insurer-hospital practices; (3) rate rationalization for TPAs; (4) timely renewal of TPA contracts; (5) fraud prevention frameworks. Management explicitly stated this will be "a new dawn" for hospitals — if implemented, private hospital pricing power will structurally improve because insurer bullying on rate renewals (which currently cap Artemis's realizable pricing growth at 3%) will be curtailed. Artemis is positioned to be a net beneficiary, not a victim, of insurance regulation.

Medical Value Travel — Geopolitical Risk Assessment

Middle East represents only 30% of Artemis's international revenue, which itself is 31% of total revenue — meaning Middle East is only ~9% of consolidated revenue. A repeat of the March 2026 war disruption (15–18% dip) would reduce consolidated revenue by approximately 1.5% on a sustained basis. Management confirmed 90% recovery by May 2026. The diversification strategy (Africa, CIS, SAARC, Canada, Nordic countries) is deliberately constructed to neutralise single-region risks. This is good risk architecture.

Equity Raise — INR 700 Cr Concern?

The Board has approved a fundraise of up to ₹700 Cr, subject to shareholders' approval. Management clarified this is not for currently-announced projects (Raipur + South Delhi can be funded through internal accruals + debt up to ₹350 Cr peak). The ₹700 Cr raise is earmarked for additional brownfield or greenfield projects currently in the pipeline. The key risks are: (1) dilution quantum — at ₹280/share, ₹700 Cr = ~25 Cr new shares = ~15% dilution to current float; (2) deployment risk — capital raised before assets are finalized could compress ROE temporarily. Management's stated intent to finalize at least one new project before raising capital is the right sequencing. Watch for the shareholder approval vote carefully.

Dividend & Shareholder Returns

Dividend yield is 0.16% — nominal. Artemis is in an aggressive reinvestment phase; dividends will remain token. Buybacks are not on the agenda. The value creation mechanism is pure earnings compounding. Given the growth runway, this is the appropriate capital allocation choice — the hurdle rate for internal investment (16–18% ROCE) far exceeds any realistic cost of equity return to investors via dividends.
